Headline numbers

These are design targets. Nothing has been measured yet; the first measurement will be the vacuum test. Each row says what the number means in plain words.

The beam

WhatTargetWhat it means
Beam energy5 to 15 kVHow hard the electrons are flung at the sample. Set by the voltage on the gun. Higher gives a finer spot but more X-rays, so 15 kV is the ceiling.
Electron sourceTungsten hairpinA V-shaped tungsten wire heated to about 2,700 K. Electrons boil off the tip. The same kind of filament commercial SEMs used for decades, bought as a consumable.
LensesTwo, magneticA condenser lens that does the first shrink of the beam, and an objective lens that forms the final focus on the sample. Both are coils in steel shells, made on the lathe.
Spot size, predicted130 to 255 nmHow small the beam can be focused with both lenses on. That is the detail the microscope can resolve. A human hair is about 70,000 nm across.
Spot size, first imageabout 3 µmWith only the objective lens running. Coarser, but it carries over a hundred times more current, which makes the first image easy to get.
Beam current0.5 nA to over 100 nAHow many electrons per second land on the sample. A smaller spot always carries less current, so the best resolution comes with the weakest signal.

The hardware

WhatTargetWhat it means
Vacuum10-5 TorrAbout one hundred-millionth of atmospheric pressure. Needed so electrons do not scatter off air, the filament does not burn out, and the high voltage does not arc. The pump bought should hold the chamber near 4 × 10-6 Torr, a little better than needed.
Turbo pumpEdwards EXT75DX, 61 L/sA turbomolecular pump, a stack of blades spinning at tens of thousands of rpm, does the hard work. Bought used, drive electronics built in, 95 mm inlet flange. See milestone 2.
Backing pumpKNF dry diaphragmTakes the chamber from atmosphere to about half a Torr and carries away the turbo's exhaust. No oil anywhere, so nothing can creep back toward the column.
GaugeInficon MPG400One gauge that reads from atmosphere down to a hundred-millionth of a Torr, by combining two sensors in one body and switching between them on its own.
Column heightabout 450 mmFrom the sample up to the top of the gun. The chamber and the pump hang below that.
Objective lens114 mm across, 95 mm tallThree turned pieces of low-carbon steel around a 1,000-turn coil. The underside is tapered to a 24 mm nose so an X-ray detector can see the sample later.
Condenser lens110 mm across, 59 mm tallSame construction, 1,500-turn coil. Both lenses fit a 12-inch lathe.
Sample chamber150 mm cube insideMachined from aluminium with bolt-on, O-ring-sealed plates, so no welding and no leak detector needed.
Samplesup to about 25 mmSit on the standard half-inch pin stubs every SEM uses. Electrically conductive samples first, such as metal parts and meshes.
Scan1024 × 1024 pixelsAt 1 microsecond per pixel, a full frame takes about a second. An FPGA steps the beam and samples the detector so every value lands in exactly the pixel it belongs to.

The goals, in order

GoalPasses when
First imageA copper mesh of known spacing is recognisable, and its measured spacing is within 10% of the real thing.
Real detectorA backscatter diode or a secondary-electron detector gives a cleaner picture than the simplest signal, and a two-metal sample shows which metal is which.
Sub-micron resolutionDetail under 1 µm resolved with both lenses running. Stretch goal: under 200 nm.
AutomationOne-command pump-down, a motorised stage, autofocus, and stitched mosaics of many frames.
Element identificationAn X-ray detector (EDS) that tells copper from aluminium by the energy of the X-rays each gives off. Budgeted and planned separately, but the chamber is designed so it fits.
